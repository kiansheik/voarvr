## Research and model (brief items 1–9)

1. **Sources:** [evidence ledger](../research/species/magpie.md) links Featherbase, AVONET, Tobalske & Dial1996, Hieronymus2016, Baier et al.2013, Lee et al.2015 and Laura Mathews. Mathews is mechanism inspiration only; this is an original generated model. Historical North American “Pica pica” flight data are comparative P. hudsonia, not European specimen measurements.
2. **Measured morphology:** AVONET European unflattened carpal-to-tip wing .1933m (n16), tail .2421m (n8), tarsus .0472m and culmen .0408m. These are not wingspan. Authored model measures .560000m span, .061706m² both-wing projected union excluding body, .242100m central rectrix; inferred humerus/forearm/manus .05582/.06773/.04232m. Derived model AR5.08, loading34.58N/m².
3. **Provenance:** .2175kg is a comparative literature mass; .56m span and anatomical proportions are inferred. Wing area is measured from inferred artwork, not a biological specimen. Joint limits, human leverage, stroke force, assistance and embodiment scale2.5 are gameplay tuning. Exact biological wingbeat frequency is unavailable, explicitly flagged; the 1Hz test is human exercise input.
4. **Skeleton:**63 bones:9 core,20 primaries,18 secondaries,12 graduated rectrices,2 alula and2 feet.2969 source vertices,4 mesh groups and one shared palette material. Duck/Dragon remain13 bones after the preceding ground milestone.
5. **Coupling:** forearm-attached secondaries follow elbow motion with modest fan; hand/digit primaries have progressive nonlinear fan. Tracked reach drives shoulder/elbow/manus fold without changing bone positions or lengths. Sweep and axial rotation are bounded; individual feathers retain independent rotations.
6. **Alula:** automatic low-speed/positive-incidence or braking deployment rotates one small feather group per wing. Aggregate physics allows up to3° tuned stall delay; no direct upward force. A deployed-alula braking regression checks that passive energy decreases.
7. **Tail:**12 graduated feathers fan for braking and fold for tuck; pitch and yaw convey braking and steering. Physics adds bounded drag and heading response. It does not solve an independent aerodynamic tail pitching moment.
8. **Human mapping:** existing calibrated two-link arm mapping remains; Magpie adds reach-dependent folding, progressive feather fan and bounded shoulder pronation. Natural head neutral, comfortable pitch and existing buttons are preserved. Both grips + left-stick click marks telemetry; right-stick click remains HUD.
9. **Architecture:** optional morphology and articulation capabilities sit beside the legacy rig. Duck uses legacy avian presentation; Dragon is membrane; Magpie uses articulated avian. Species geometry and gameplay leverage remain separate. Future hummingbird/bat/insect differences are documented in [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md), not implemented.
