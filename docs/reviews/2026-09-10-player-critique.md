# VoarVR: independent player, embodiment and replayability critique

Reviewed 2026-09-10 against working tree `54562d1`, including the uncommitted advanced-neutral change. This is a source, recorded-motion and screenshot review, not a headset playthrough by the critic. No runtime code, asset, scene or editor state was changed. Scores evaluate what the evidence actually exposes; felt comfort, exertion and enjoyment remain unverified.

**Verdict:** VoarVR has a distinctive embodied flight foundation and evidence that a wearer can now learn to soar and trigger an actual trajectory-based loop. Its largest product gap is the journey around flight: teaching, readable success, varied reasons to fly, and preserving progress when the player rests or recovers. Another species or a larger world would currently add less value than a small, satisfying expedition with clear feedback and a reason to return.

## Provisional grades

Scale: 3 = important pieces missing; 5 = functional prototype; 7 = convincing, coherent slice; 9 = exceptional execution. These are critic judgments, not measured user averages or a commercial release review.

## Findings and precise changes

### P1. Make flight understandable, recoverable and comfortable enough to choose

**F1 — High / unverified risk: advanced flight still lacks current wearer acceptance.** `AcrobaticFlight.cs` now suppresses passive pitch at calibrated zero and bounds opposing pitch torque. This directly addresses a strong recorded mechanism. In the preceding capture, valid airborne advanced angular-speed magnitude p95 reaches the configured cap: 80°/s Duck and 110°/s Magpie. These magnitudes include intentional maneuvers; they are not proof of unintended rotation. Full-rotation first-person is explicitly implemented in `FlightCamera.FirstPersonBasis`; the inverted-eye screenshot confirms its consequence.

Keep the requested embodied view available. Expose camera style independently from control skill in a deliberate setting, with a preview of what “full rotation” means; do not force advanced players into a comfort camera or require inversion for campaign progress. Verify relaxed neutral and small commanded movements before increasing advanced challenge demands. Add a way to leave a maneuver and continue the current journey without requiring a restart. Optional horizon reference should be user-selected and evaluated on device, not silently added to the quiet view.

**F2 — High / objective design gap: recovery is coupled to losing the current attempt.** `BirdFlightDriver.ShowMode` calls A “RECOVER”; `RestartAndCalibrate` resets origin/controller, returns to third person and calls `Expedition.Restart`. `ExpeditionDirector.Tick` also starts a fresh challenge on reset. The current behavior is deliberate and documented, but a player seeking a comfortable grip mid-journey can lose their stage. Only completed results are persisted; there is no resumable in-progress expedition.

Retain the existing A contract until a reviewed input change is accepted. First add explicit pause actions: **Recalibrate here**, **Return to last safe perch**, **Restart route**, **Save and leave**. Explain their effects before invocation, preserve earned route milestones, and use contact-safe recovery positions. Platform recenter already offers in-place calibration; make the distinction learnable without requiring knowledge of a reserved system gesture. A recovery should not award altitude, quiet gain, catches or tricks from the repositioning itself.

**F3 — High / objective coverage gap with unverified usability impact: Training is a soaring challenge, not an onboarding sequence.** `FlightChallenge.Kind` gives Training only DiscoverLift/Soar. The gesture is a 0.7s left-stick hold; advanced, pause and view coaching in `BirdFlightDriver` is textual and `FlightCamera` suppresses it with HUD off. `ExpeditionDirector.LateUpdate` hides the destination beacon as well. Respect the user's quiet-cockpit requirement: re-enabling constant text is not the fix.

Build a short optional learning perch, with one demonstrated action at a time: comfortable calibration, wing/body mapping, gentle launch, turn, enter lift, read net climb, land, pause, recover and leave. Use a nearby demonstrator bird or recorded wing pose, concise optional speech/subtitles and one unmistakable spatial target. Allow skipping/revisiting each lesson. Offer optional on-demand help while paused. Add brief distinct audio/haptic acknowledgements for mode/view changes and pause/resume so a silent HUD need not mean an unacknowledged action.

**F4 — Medium / objective cue ambiguity: rising air and successful soaring sound too similar.** `FlightFeedback.Tick` keys the repeating thermal tone to `WindVelocity.y > 2`; it can sound while the player descends in rising air. `SnackCue` reuses the same `thermalTone` clip. The old Dragon capture showed real ascent that the wearer did not recognize; source now gives a net-climb text message, but that is intentionally absent in the quiet view.

Give three events different signatures: entering useful air, sustaining actual net climb, and leaving/failing to hold lift. Use real climb plus sampled air and hysteresis to avoid chatter at boundaries. Let gently converging motes reveal the center and an optional variometer make net climb audible; set the center cue apart from the moth collection signature. Test audio muted, visuals muted and both together. Do not increase raw thermal force merely to compensate for unreadable feedback.

**F5 — Medium / unverified accessibility risk: neutral, reach and camera settings are not separate player capabilities.** `BirdTrackingCalibration` requires a tracked two-hand span of 0.7–2.2m, opposite-side placement and a level pose. The prompt says T pose, although the actual validator admits some relaxed positions. `TrackedBodyFrame` estimates heading from the spread-hand axis, which can become ambiguous during asymmetric/crossed poses. Beginner still uses calibrated head pitch, while advanced uses wrist pitch. There is no reviewed in-game accessibility setup for reach, seated use or an alternative pitch source.

Create a capability screen before flight: comfortable reach demonstration, neutral capture confirmation and a brief turn/reach check. Offer a tested reduced-range profile and seated mode before advertising either. Separate input range, physical effort scaling, camera rotation and challenge difficulty; one “easy” toggle cannot express these needs. Verify shorter/taller users and asymmetric motions before lowering the hard minimum span. Do not claim one-hand support merely by bypassing tracking checks: it needs a deliberate mapping and tests.

### P2. Give one expedition an engaging effort, discovery and recovery rhythm

**F6 — High / objective mismatch with this brief: the main medal score discourages the exercise the user wants.** `FlightChallenge.Complete` starts from 1000, subtracts two points for each second above a 0.5N active-stroke threshold, subtracts collision cost, subtracts elapsed-time cost after ten minutes, and awards at most three trick bonuses. Efficient soaring is a valid mastery objective. It cannot also act as the universal measure of a successful active session. The threshold measures simulated stroke force, not the wearer's exertion.

Keep a clearly labeled **Soaring efficiency** medal for that activity. Add parallel, noncompetitive session accomplishments for chosen movement blocks, new skills, exploration and return visits. Present a choice of route rhythm, such as gentle exploration, balanced migration and optional active challenges. Do not reward maximum repetitions, raw arm speed or calories inferred from controllers. Prefer consistent, controlled strokes and enjoyable tasks that incidentally require movement. Different species should offer different feel, not coerce the player into the physically hardest one for better rewards.

**F7 — High / objective design gap: rest has no durable place in the journey.** `ExpeditionDirector.Tick` excludes paused/streaming-blocked state but still calls `FlightChallenge.Step` while perched, and `Elapsed` increments before stage logic. After ten minutes this can reduce medals for perching. `FlightEffort` classifies slow hands after three seconds as Resting; `FlightTelemetry.Capture` excludes perched/pause frames from that calculation. A motionless spread-arm glide can still require effort. These flags must not become a claim of recovery.

Create attractive rest perches that save progress and offer a clear continue/finish choice. Perched/rest time should not cost the ordinary journey score. Preserve optional timed trials as a separately explained activity with their own clock rules. Session summaries should distinguish active hand movement, quiet airborne time, perched time, pause time and unavailable tracking coverage. Use the wearer's optional effort/discomfort report for playtest interpretation. Finishing after one chapter must feel complete; continuing should be an invitation with no expiring reward or streak loss.

**F8 — Medium / objective content limitation: two expedition names largely share one sequence.** Current Skyward is discover lift → soar → arch → land; Ridge inserts a migration waypoint. Destinations are generated from the same island/region system, and the current progress model stores only best integers plus a boolean unlock derived from Skyward score. Endless terrain supplies distance but does not automatically supply discovery.

Author one recognizable loop of three short chapters with a safe perch between them: learn the valley current, carry a seed to a damaged garden, then escort a small flock to the ridge. Each chapter gets a distinct landmark, one new flight decision and a visible consequence. Let the player choose a scenic slower route or a skill route. Persist chapter completion and discovered perches. Save broad campaigns, procedural quest generators and large collections until this route is replayed voluntarily by testers.

**F9 — Medium / subjective art-to-play preference: the world gives little evidence of inhabitants responding to the bird.** The inspected terrace has repeatable architecture, flowers and a landing surface but no visible host, changing garden state or invitation to act. Lowland and islands share broad muted shapes; these screenshots alone do not show whether a player can identify a destination from its silhouette. The authored-eyed view is clear but has little self-body reference in the inspected forward frame; wings may be visible outside it and must be checked through normal head movements.

Prioritize a few behavioral assets: one expressive guide/flock bird, one shelter/perch with clear landing language, one garden that changes when helped, one landmark with an unmistakable silhouette and one species-specific wing/foot contact animation. Give scale through nearby leaves, a companion, shadows and reliable ground contact. Retain open sightlines for wing movement and flight decisions. A cosmetic feather brushing foliage can be accompanied by a small cue; do not add full feather collision before proving it improves agency without tracking/contact conflicts.

### P3. Turn flight mastery into replay value

**F10 — Medium / objective feedback defect: trick feedback displays cumulative score as if it were the award.** `BirdFlightDriver.Tick` sets `CoachStatus` to the trick name plus `Controller.Tricks.Score`, the running total. A second 250-point trick would display +500, not its 250-point delta. `ExpeditionDirector` separately describes trick +75, capped at three, while `TrickDetector` assigns 100/250-point skill scores. These are distinct systems whose meaning is not explained to the player; feedback is hidden entirely when HUD off.

Use a single award event carrying trick type, event points, total points and challenge contribution. Show the event delta correctly in optional text, and use a short distinct cue with no dependency on HUD. Label any two scores clearly in results or consolidate them. First improve the existing four tricks before adding tricks whose classification is hard to explain.

**F11 — Medium / objective coaching gap: demanding tricks exist before their learning path.** `TrickDetector` checks body rotation, trajectory winding for loops, upright completion and bounded episodes; those are meaningful safeguards. A wearer earned a BackLoop in the latest Magpie recording. There is no actual energy-management lesson, entry corridor, recovery instruction or practice checkpoint in the current challenge state machine.

Add a safe practice area above a generous recovery floor, entered voluntarily. Teach a slalom and a full roll before loops. Demonstrate entry speed/height, one controlled input and a clear exit. Use a ghost trajectory or sparse gates; never move the headset camera to demonstrate. Report a missed attempt as one actionable cue: insufficient entry energy, interrupted rotation, or recovery before completion. Then add banked figure-eight, precision glide, smooth flare landing and thermal transfer challenges that work in stabilized view. Inverted holds remain optional and need no story unlock dependency.

**F12 — Medium / objective replay limitation: catches generate points but few strategic decisions.** `SkyForaging` maintains 24 moth slots around the player, catches within a generous swept 2.2m radius and respawns after 25s. `ForagingScore` rewards catches less than six seconds apart, capped at five. Current catches work and provide a small immediate reward, but they are not tied to a collection, route consequence or meaningful choice. Frequent omnidirectional moths can also compete with golden lift seeds.

Start with three authored foraging routes: broad gentle arcs, a turn-and-glide choice, and an optional precision chain near a safe perch. Make the flight path readable and preserve generous catches; difficulty should come from choosing and steering a route. Tie one collection result to a visible garden response or nest decoration. Keep a missed chain low-stakes and never make pausing erase banked rewards. Distinguish edible moth silhouette/motion from atmospheric motes without adding a second layer of permanent text.

**F13 — Medium / subjective progression preference: collection should reflect memories and mastery.** A small migration journal can record discovered perches, a best landing, an observed species and a completed route with a local thumbnail. Unlock feather markings, nest decorations or new route weather variants through different competencies. A route's new variant should change decisions, not merely require more repetitions. An optional local personal ghost is a stronger first replay feature than multiplayer infrastructure.

**F14 — Low / objective documentation drift: agents can accidentally restore old behavior.** `design/FLIGHT_GAME_V1.md` still describes 140m quiet gain and old rate caps 155/80/230; current source uses 100m for non-Training and caps 80/60/110. Older sections of `design/INPUT.md` describe a stabilized camera in both views and older head-pitch ranges; the later quiet refinement and source disagree with those sections. Update contracts around a single current configuration before delegating tuning. This review deliberately did not edit shared design/agent pages.

## Next wearer study

Recruit a small mix of new and experienced VR users; 6–8 is a practical first usability pass, not a statistically representative population. Record the exact APK/source stamp and personal control/view preferences. Start with a brief calibration/comfort check, then one short learning route, an optional second chapter, and optional trick practice only for willing participants. Stop the activity whenever the wearer wants; do not target exercising through discomfort. Longer sessions are evidence only when freely chosen.

Capture observer notes synchronized with telemetry markers and ask brief ratings after chapters: enjoyment, control confidence, feeling like the bird, visual clarity, perceived effort and any discomfort. Ask what they wanted to do next and what made them stop. Do not substitute hand speed or time-in-headset for those answers. Distinguish intentional reset, unintentional input, exploration and confusion with observer/wearer annotation; telemetry alone cannot label intent.

Proposed release-to-next-slice gates: at least 80% of first-time testers complete the basic teaching route without verbal rescue; every tester can find pause/exit; no repeated forced wrist/neck posture; no loss of banked progress when stopping; median reported enjoyment and control confidence at least 7/10. Treat any recurring discomfort or navigation trap as a high-priority revision regardless of the average. These are internal decision thresholds, not medical or scientific claims.

Also inspect actual on-device stereo views during turn, bank, landing, recovery and an optional loop; check frame presentation separately with the technical reviewer. Compare new captures to the pre-neutral torque window without treating an arbitrary stable interval as automatic calibration. Repeat the focused loop once after revision, using new images/captures rather than rescoring old evidence.

The best long-session outcome is a player who completes a satisfying chapter, rests, then chooses another because the next flight promises something new. Build that repeatable choice before building a larger quantity of scenery.
