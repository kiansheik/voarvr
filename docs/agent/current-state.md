# Current state

Updated 2026-09-09. The repository has a playable Duck/Dragon flight prototype with procedural scenery and moving wind.

BirdFlight supports Duck and Dragon, selected through the runtime CharacterSelect menu. Both use reproducible Blender -> FBX assets with the same nine-bone wing contract and four skinned renderers. Dragon now has an approximately 7 m span, continuous blended membrane skinning, connected spars/fingers, and a species wing-area multiplier of 5.5 (6.16 m² effective area). The chase camera fits its full span; the first-person anchor is raised above its back spines. Duck keeps its original profile.

Latest worn feedback: Dragon art is much improved but v2 flight was exhausting and unusable. The v3 fix preserves size/mass/wing lift and slower attitude, normalizes active stroke acceleration to human effort, converts neutral downstroke work into forward thrust, and caps hand-speed force. Moderate controller arcs (36cm peak-to-peak,0.8Hz) sustain flight in still air and launch from ground; this is simulated evidence awaiting a new worn test.

Right primary **A restarts and calibrates together** with a level comfortable0.7–2.2m spread. Platform Meta recenter now **recalibrates in place**, preserving position/velocity/view and waiting for0.35s stable fresh tracking. Stability is measured against the beginning of the hold window including controller orientation, not only consecutive-frame distance. The reserved Meta button is not directly bound. Right secondary B toggles view, left secondary Y cycles weather, X pauses, and **left Menu returns to CharacterSelect** (Escape/gamepad East on desktop).

Flight remains deterministic bounded force integration with gravity, lift/drag, bank, stall, tuck, flare, strokes, landing/perching and flap takeoff. Moving thermals, crosswind, turbulence and a Wild-mode downdraft now affect air-relative velocity. Assisted/Touring/Wild/StillAir modes trade help against effort/turbulence. A no-flap circling Dragon regression gains altitude in moving thermals. These are gameplay parameters, not biological claims; angular momentum, general scenery collision and ground effect are not modeled.

The procedural playground builds a bounded city/forest corridor with tiered towers, varied crowns, perch beacons, practice arches, hills and a river. Animated thermal curls and traveling wind traces share simulation time and wind sampling with flight. Surface shading adds directional shape and distance haze. Scenery is still simple procedural prototype geometry, not a finished landscape or endless terrain. Thermal markers cover only the low-altitude course although thermal physics extends above them.
